This is a group of students working together using non-digital tools such as colored papers, scissors, cardboard, and other art materials. These traditional tools are used to create a project through hands-on activities. Each member is actively involved in cutting, shaping, and assembling materials, which promotes creativity and practical skills.

Non-digital tools are important because they allow learners to experience direct interaction with materials, improving their motor skills, focus, and imagination. Even without the use of technology, the group was able to produce meaningful output through cooperation and effort. This shows that learning can still be effective and engaging using simple, traditional methods.
