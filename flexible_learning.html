What is Flexible Learning?

The image shows a classroom demonstration where different learning tools are used to support teaching and engagement. The presenters used a combination of traditional and digital tools, such as printed visual aids (charts, posters, and flashcards) and digital technology like a projector and presentation slides. This setup reflects flexible learning because it allows information to be delivered in multiple ways visual, auditory, and interactive. The projector helps present organized content clearly, while the printed materials reinforce key ideas and make the lesson more engaging for learners. The use of a speaker also enhances communication, ensuring that everyone can hear and understand the presentation. Overall, the integration of these tools shows how flexibility in teaching methods can improve understanding, participation, and learning outcomes. It demonstrates that combining both traditional and digital tools creates a more effective and inclusive learning environment.

🏫 Face-to-Face Learning

The traditional classroom setting where students and teacher are physically present. In PE, this is ideal for hands-on activities, skill demonstrations, and real-time feedback on movement and form.

  • Direct teacher observation and correction
  • Team sports, relay races, and group drills
  • Use of school equipment: cones, balls, hoops
  • Immediate feedback on technique and safety
In-GymReal-TimeEquipment-Ready

📄 Modular Distance Learning

Students receive printed or digital activity modules and complete PE tasks at home at their own pace. This is the most common flexible mode used in the Philippines, especially in areas with limited internet access.

  • Printed activity sheets with illustrated exercises
  • Self-paced workout plans and fitness logs
  • Photo or video submissions for assessment
  • Weekly modules aligned with PE competencies
Self-PacedOfflineLow-Tech

💻 Online Learning

PE is delivered through digital platforms where students access lessons, videos, and assessments online. Teachers use video calls, LMS platforms, and fitness apps to guide students remotely.

  • Live or recorded workout video sessions
  • Google Classroom for distributing tasks
  • Kahoot for PE knowledge quizzes
  • Fitness apps to log activity and progress
Internet-RequiredDigital ToolsAsynchronous

🔀 Blended Learning

A hybrid approach combining face-to-face and online or modular instruction. Students attend some classes in person while completing other tasks digitally or at home.

  • In-school sessions for skills and sports
  • Online modules for theory and reflection
  • Flexible scheduling for diverse learners
  • Combines digital and non-digital tools
HybridFlexible ScheduleMixed Media

🏠 Home-Based PE

PE activities are specially designed for safe performance at home with no special equipment needed. Teachers create routines using bodyweight exercises, dance, stretching, and active games families can do together.

  • Bodyweight exercises: jumping jacks, planks, squats
  • Dance workouts using YouTube tutorials
  • Family-inclusive fitness challenges
  • Journals to track daily physical activity
